# overview

annealogy is a cognitive translation tool. you give it a concept you do not understand, and it explains that concept using the mental models you already use to make sense of the world.

it is not a tutor. it is not a flashcard app. it does not try to be comprehensive. it tries to make ideas click.

## what it is

a small, focused app with one job: take a concept and translate it into the user's chosen analogy system, then show the explanation in a strict five-part structure.

every explanation contains exactly five sections, in this order:

1. analogy. a short, intuitive pass in the user's chosen system.
2. mapping. an explicit list pairing each analogy element with the real concept component.
3. visual. a clean structured diagram of how the parts relate.
4. real explanation. the actual concept in proper terms, but grounded by the analogy above.
5. limits. an honest note on where the analogy stops working.

skipping or reordering any section breaks the product.

## who it is for

people who already think in patterns. people who learn faster when an idea is anchored to something they already understand. people who are tired of generic ai answers that read like a textbook.

if you have ever said "wait, is it kind of like..." in the middle of someone else's explanation and immediately understood the rest, this is built for you.

## what problem it solves

most explanations start with a definition. definitions are useless to someone who does not yet know what they are looking at. annealogy starts where you already are: a system you understand. it then walks you to the new concept by showing what is the same and what is different.

it also forces honesty. the limits section means you never finish an explanation believing the analogy more than the actual thing.

## how it feels to use

quiet, soft, intentional. warm paper background, dusty rose accent, no animations that distract. you type a concept, pick a system, and read five tight sections that fit on one screen. you can switch the system and re-read the same idea through another lens.

it should feel like a personal thinking journal that explains things back to you, not like a chat with a robot.
